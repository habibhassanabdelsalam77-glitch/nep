Top cultural traditions in Chad:

Traditional music with drums and string instruments.
Camel racing in desert regions, Colorful clothing (boubou, turbans).
Strong oral storytelling traditions, Hospitality to guests (sharing meals, tea ceremonies).

National holidays in Chad:

January 1 New Year's Day
April 13 National Day (independence)
August 11 Independence Day (1960)
December 1 Freedom and Democracy Day
Islamic holidays (Eid al-Fitr, Eid al-Adha)vary by year.

History

Chad has been home to ancient civilizations for thousands of years.
In the past, powerful kingdoms like Kanem-Bornu ruled the region. In the 1900s, Chad became a French colony. The country gained independence on August 11, 1960.

People

Chad has more than 200 ethnic groups, making it one of the most diverse countries in Africa. The largest groups include the Sara, Arab, Kanembu, and Toubou. People in Chad are known for their strong traditions,
music, and hospitality.

Language

French and Arabic are the official languages. More than 120 local languages and dialects are spoken. Common local languages include Sara (in the south) and Chadian Arabic (widely spoken across the country).



Quick Facts about CHAD

  • Capital City: N'Djamena
  • Population: ~18 million (2025 est.)
  • Official Language: French and Arabic
  • Currency: Central African CFA franc (XAF)
  • Location: Central Africa (bordered by Libya, Sudan, CAR, Cameroon, Nigeria, Niger)
  • Climate: Mostly desert (north), savanna (south)
  • Major Lake: Lake Chad (once one of the largest lakes in the world)
  • Highest Point: Emi Koussi (3,445 m) in the Tibesti Mountains
  • Famous National Park: Zakouma National Park
  • Nickname: The Dead Heart of Africa” (because it lies at the center of the continent)
